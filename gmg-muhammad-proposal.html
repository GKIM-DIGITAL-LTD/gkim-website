Partnership Structure

Four Models, One Evolution

The partnership is designed to grow in depth and integration over time, moving through distinct phases as trust and shared infrastructure develop.

Recommended First Step
Model A
Preferred Clinical Partner

Muhammad's business remains fully independent. GMG becomes his preferred clinical delivery partner — low risk, fast to launch, and commercially straightforward for both parties.

Marketing → Patient qualification → GMG evaluates → Medical necessity → Prescription → Supplier fulfils → GMG follows patient
Revenue Allocation
Muhammad earns from DME fulfilment
GMG earns professional fees, bills insurance, gains longitudinal patients
Phase Two
Model B
Virtual PCP Platform

Patients no longer arrive for a brace or CGM — they arrive for healthcare. The initial DME need opens a broader health journey: annual wellness, fall risk, diabetes, hypertension, medications, behavioural health, genetics, cancer screening, and preventive care.

Value Driver
The shift: from one-time DME transaction to lifetime patient management — aligning directly with GMG's existing strategic direction.
Long-Term Integration
Model C
Clinical Infrastructure Partner

Muhammad effectively outsources his entire clinical operation to GMG. He focuses entirely on commercial growth, marketing, supplier relationships, and new markets. GMG provides everything else — staff, technology, scheduling, documentation, billing, compliance, and analytics.

Commercial Structure
Recurring infrastructure fee + activity-based clinical services
Stickiness: replacing GMG would require replacing an entire operating platform
Highest Long-Term Value
Model D
Strategic Channel Alliance

Muhammad becomes a channel partner who introduces other organisations needing clinical infrastructure — not just a customer. His entrepreneurial background across genetics, DME, telemarketing, and patient acquisition makes this a natural extension.

Potential Referral Sectors
Includes: DME companies, diagnostic labs, genetic testing, RPM providers, medical devices, digital therapeutics, chronic disease programmes, employer health
