Precision Medical Group · Compliance Architecture

Patient admission without compromising clinical independence

A controlled hand-off from informed marketing interest to independent medical care.

The AI Patient Support Specialist / Patient Care Coordinator verifies identity, patient understanding, voluntary intent and acquisition compliance before a patient enters Precision Medical Group's clinical workflow. It does not diagnose, determine medical necessity, select a laboratory, or approve coverage.

Core boundary
CLEAR means the patient may enter the clinical workflow. It does not mean the patient has qualified for a test, treatment, prescription, insurance benefit, AWV, or laboratory service.
Shared control model

One system, separate responsibilities

Marketing expectations are defined in advance, independently tested with the patient, recorded, monitored and fed back into governance.

  • MG: creates informed interest, names Precision Medical Group (PMG), and explains the immediate-care and optional longitudinal-care model.
  • AI PSS: verifies identity, PMG awareness, purpose, voluntary intent, existing-PCP context, acquisition provenance and compliance.
  • Clinician: assesses the issue that brought the patient to PMG and independently determines diagnosis, treatment, testing and follow-up where appropriate.
  • Longitudinal care: may be offered after the encounter. It is optional and is not a condition of today's assessment.
  • Laboratory: performs testing only after appropriate clinical ordering.
Design principle
MGs create informed interest. The AI PSS establishes informed care entry. Clinicians determine medical necessity.

Marketing Groups

Create informed interest, not medical demand

Before transfer, the patient should know the medical practice is Precision Medical Group, understand why they are being connected, know PMG is independent of the Marketing Group, and understand that PMG can complement rather than automatically replace an existing bricks-and-mortar PCP.

BLUE — Required / permitted

  • Name Precision Medical Group (PMG).
  • Explain PMG is an independent primary-care medical practice, separate from the MG.
  • Use approved educational materials and explain the service accurately.
  • Explain PMG may complement and, when appropriate and authorised, collaborate with the patient's existing PCP.
  • Explain today's encounter is to assess the issue that brought the patient to PMG.
  • Explain diagnosis, treatment, testing and follow-up depend on the PMG clinician's independent assessment.
  • Explain ongoing PMG primary care is optional and can be considered later.
  • Confirm the patient voluntarily wants to speak with PMG.

RED — Prohibited

  • Tell a patient they “qualify” for a test or service.
  • Guarantee Medicare, insurance coverage or zero out-of-pocket cost.
  • Tell the patient what the clinician will diagnose, prescribe or order.
  • Give clinical advice or determine medical necessity.
  • Say the patient must change or abandon an existing PCP.
  • Represent PMG as already being the patient's PCP merely because today's encounter was accepted.
  • Pressure the patient to enrol in longitudinal care or make it a condition of today's care.
  • Promise a specific laboratory.
  • Coach the patient on how to answer the AI PSS.

Expected patient understanding

“I understand I am being connected to Precision Medical Group to discuss my health. PMG can work alongside my existing doctor if I have one. I understand the clinician decides what is medically appropriate, and I do not have to sign up for ongoing PMG care today.”
After transfer

The PSS independently tests what actually happened

  • Does the patient know this is Precision Medical Group?
  • Can the patient explain, in their own words, why they are on the call?
  • Do they understand PMG is independent of the MG?
  • Do they understand PMG may complement rather than replace an existing PCP?
  • Do they understand longitudinal care is optional?
  • Were promises, inducements, coaching or predetermined outcomes introduced?
Do not coach to the test
MGs receive the standards and required educational outcomes, but not the PSS's exact gate logic, trigger phrases, thresholds or required answers.
MG conduct while PSS questions the patient
The AI controls the compliance portion. Questions directed to the patient must be answered by the patient. If the MG answers, prompts, coaches or materially interrupts, the AI instructs the MG not to intervene and repeats the question. Repeated or substantive interference is a compliance event and may cause Acquisition Attestation to fail.
PMG clinicians

The PSS protects the encounter; it does not replace it

By clinical hand-off, the system should have independently established patient identity, voluntary intent, awareness of PMG, acquisition provenance, the patient's relationship to any existing PCP, and relevant marketing concerns.

What the clinician may rely on administratively

  • Identity has been checked.
  • The patient knows they are interacting with Precision Medical Group.
  • The patient has voluntarily chosen to continue.
  • The patient understands PMG may complement an existing PCP.
  • The patient understands ongoing PMG care is optional.
  • Acquisition provenance and material RED-rule signals have been recorded.
Clinical independence

What remains entirely clinical

  • Diagnosis and differential diagnosis.
  • Medical necessity.
  • Whether testing is appropriate.
  • Whether medication or treatment is appropriate.
  • Which test, if any, should be ordered.
  • Whether another form or level of care is more appropriate.
Immediate to longitudinal
“Precision Medical Group also offers ongoing primary care if you would like continued support after today's issue. You do not need to decide about that now, and choosing not to receive ongoing care will not affect today's medical assessment.” Conversational variation is acceptable if this meaning is preserved.
Clinical urgency is separate
Urgent or emergency routing is not a compliance DECLINE. Clinical safety routing is represented independently.
Laboratories

Acquisition, medicine and laboratory services remain separated

  • Patient acquisition is independent of the laboratory.
  • The AI PSS does not authorise laboratory testing.
  • Medical necessity remains with the treating clinician.
  • Patients are not promised a particular laboratory during marketing.
  • Commercial arrangements do not determine clinical ordering.
  • Orders arise only after appropriate clinical assessment.

AI PSS / PCC

Four hard gates, plus mandatory education checks

Identity → Purpose & Voluntary Intent → Acquisition Attestation → Care-Entry Eligibility
Care-Entry Eligibility is administrative/non-clinical only. It never determines medical necessity, test eligibility, treatment eligibility, insurance coverage or entitlement to a reimbursable service.

Patient-admission sequence

  1. Identity: establish that the person is the patient or appropriately authorised representative.
  2. PMG awareness: ask unaided which medical practice the patient expected to speak with.
  3. Purpose: ask in the patient's own words what they expect PMG to help with today.
  4. Acquisition provenance: establish how the patient came to PMG and what they were told, before substantial corrective education.
  5. Voluntary intent: confirm knowing and voluntary desire to continue.
  6. Existing PCP: establish existing PCP context and explain PMG may complement rather than automatically replace that relationship.
  7. Clinical expectation: confirm that diagnosis, testing, treatment and follow-up depend on independent clinical assessment.
  8. Longitudinal option: explain ongoing PMG care is optional, need not be chosen now, and declining it does not affect today's assessment.
  9. RED-rule checks: test for promises, inducements, coaching, predetermined services or misleading coverage claims.
  10. Corrective education: where genuinely correctable, educate and reverify without overwriting the original answer.
  11. Disposition: resolve to CLEAR or DECLINE; HOLD is transient only.

Ask → record → assess → educate → reverify

Open-ended questions come before correction. The system records what the patient arrived believing, then separately records what the patient understands after PMG education.

Do not remediate away evidence
Corrective education never erases or overwrites the original response. A patient becoming adequately informed later does not change the historical acquisition event.
Separate patient and MG outcomes
A patient may ultimately CLEAR after an innocent misunderstanding while the MG still receives a compliance event. Conversely, patient CLEAR does not prove the preceding marketing interaction was compliant.

Policy boundary
The AI must not invent severity at runtime. Which RED events are recordable-only versus admission-invalidating must be expressly defined in approved legal/compliance policy.

Annual Wellness Visit language

An AWV is an encounter/service concept, not a predetermined laboratory test. Generic compliance language should not seed an expectation of a specific reimbursable encounter unless the patient actually entered through an AWV-specific pathway.

Default wording
“The PMG clinician will assess your individual circumstances and determine what care is appropriate.”
End-to-end acquisition

One RED/BLUE source of truth

The same policy governs all pre-clinical patient communications: MG humans, MG AI, PMG outbound AI, PMG inbound PSS, SMS, landing pages and prerecorded messaging. Roles may have different permissions, but factual and compliance rules remain canonical.

Outbound audit required
Objection handling, cost/coverage reassurance and urgency language must be audited against the same RED/BLUE policy so outbound engagement never teaches expectations that inbound compliance later treats as suspicious.
