The problem

Most systems fail before they are built

01
Incomplete requirements
What the business needs is never fully captured before development begins.
02
Inconsistent workflow logic
Processes are designed in isolation. Integration reveals gaps only under pressure.
03
Stakeholder misalignment
Different teams hold different mental models of the same system.
04
Complexity discovered too late
Operational edge cases emerge during build, not before — doubling the cost.
05
Validation happens in code
Business logic is tested through software, not through structured thinking beforehand.
06
AI is added, not designed
Automation is layered onto broken processes. The output is faster chaos.

This is not a development problem. It is a system definition problem — and it is solvable before a single line of code is written.

What we work through

Six dimensions
of business design

Each session is structured around the six layers of a functioning business system — from vision through automation.

◎
Business Engine
How your business creates, delivers, and captures value — defined as a system, not an idea.
⇄
Workflow Architecture
The sequence of operations that makes your model function. Where work flows, where it breaks.
⊹
Decision Logic
The conditional rules that govern how your business responds to inputs, exceptions, and edge cases.
◫
Data & State Logic
What gets tracked, why it matters, and how state changes move through your system over time.
◈
KPI Structure
The metrics that prove the system is working — designed from first principles, not convention.
⟡
AI-Native Opportunity
Where intelligence should be embedded versus where human judgement is the actual competitive advantage.
