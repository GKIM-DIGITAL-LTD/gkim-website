GEARS White Paper No. 3

The Business Evolution Platform

Reference Architecture for the Agentic Change Management System

Managers manage investigations, evidence, experiments and GEAR evolution — not AI agents.

Section 1 · Context

From Inquiry Engine to Platform

White Paper No. 2 defined the Agentic Change Management System as an architectural inquiry engine — a system whose primary outputs are structured investigations, evidence profiles, and governed architectural decisions rather than completed tasks.

This paper defines what the platform that runs that engine looks like. It specifies a reference architecture — the layered structure of the Business Evolution Platform — along with the Manager UX, the core object model, the knowledge provider interface, and the agent capability taxonomy. It does not specify a technology stack. The reference architecture is intentionally execution-agnostic: the principles and structures described here should be implementable across a wide range of technical choices.

Design Mandate

The platform must make managers feel they are doing their most important work — guiding the architectural evolution of their enterprise — not managing a fleet of AI agents.

Section 2 · Core Thesis

Managers Govern Investigations, Not Agents

The most important design decision in the Business Evolution Platform is also the most counterintuitive one: the platform does not surface AI agents as first-class objects in the manager experience. Agents are infrastructure. What managers interact with — what they prioritise, challenge, approve, and learn from — are investigations.

This is not a cosmetic choice. Platforms that surface agents as first-class objects create a cognitive model in which humans are managing AI behaviour: reviewing agent outputs, approving agent actions, correcting agent errors. This is both exhausting and brittle. It recreates the micromanagement dynamic that good managers have learned to avoid with human teams — now applied, at scale, to automated systems.

When a manager opens the platform each morning, she does not see what the agents did overnight. She sees what the business learned, what proposals are awaiting her judgment, and what is at risk.

Agent-Centric Design (avoid)

The manager sees agents, their statuses, their queues, their errors. She spends her time understanding what the agents did and correcting their behaviour. Her mental model is: I am managing a fleet of AI workers.

Investigation-Centric Design (adopt)

The manager sees investigations, their evidence states, their confidence levels, their proximity to a governance decision. Her mental model is: I am guiding the architectural evolution of my enterprise.

Agents are named and visible in the platform — specifically in the Investigation Workspace, where their contributions to the evidence record and their challenge positions in negotiation are surfaced. But they are contributors to investigations, not subjects of management attention.

Section 3 · Reference Architecture

The Platform Layer Stack

The Business Evolution Platform is structured as six horizontal layers. Each layer has a distinct responsibility, and the interfaces between layers are defined and stable — allowing individual layers to be replaced or upgraded without requiring changes to adjacent layers.

Business Evolution Platform · Six-Layer Reference Architecture · Execution-Agnostic

Layer 1 · Manager UX

The interface through which managers interact with investigations, evidence, proposals, governance decisions, and the GEAR topology. Investigation-centric; agents are infrastructure. Twelve primary views.

Layer 2 · ACMS Control Plane

The fourteen-stage pipeline defined in WP2. Manages investigation state, routes between stages, enforces confidence thresholds, and triggers governance gates. Stateful and event-driven.

Layer 3 · Agent Runtime

The execution environment for investigative agents. Provides capability sandboxing, tool access, evidence ledger write permissions, and KAS execution authority. Agents do not have direct GEAR write access — only proposals do.

Layer 4 · Knowledge Integration

The common evidence interface through which Knowledge Providers — internal systems, external data, domain sources — expose structured evidence to the ACMS. All evidence carries provenance, freshness, and weighting metadata.

Layer 5 · GEAR Graph Store

The persistent store of the GEARS Blueprint — the versioned, append-only record of every GEAR, its configuration, its relationships, and its change history. The VGQL query layer provides traversal and diagnostic access.

Layer 6 · Business Knowledge OS

The Business Knowledge Stack from WP1: the six-layer architecture from Industry Intelligence to Simulation Models. The BKS is the ACMS's primary evidence source and the GEAR graph's semantic context.

Every object belongs to a GEAR — either directly (a Trigger is raised against a GEAR), or through a chain of ownership (a Hypothesis belongs to an Investigation which targets a GEAR).

Section 5 · Design Principles

Seven Platform Principles

The following principles are not guidelines — they are architectural constraints that every implementation of the Business Evolution Platform must satisfy. A platform that violates any one of them cannot correctly be called an implementation of this reference architecture.

Principle 1

Every Object Belongs to a GEAR

Every Trigger, Investigation, Evidence item, Hypothesis, Proposal, and Decision is owned by — and indexed against — a GEAR. There are no platform-wide objects that float free of the GEAR topology.

Principle 2

Recursive Diagnosis Is Mandatory

No Investigation may produce a Change Proposal without first completing a recursive GEAR diagnosis — traversing both upstream (causal) and downstream (consequential) from the target GEAR to establish blast radius.

Principle 3

Temporary Working Graphs

Each Investigation builds its own temporary knowledge sub-graph from relevant BKS fragments. This graph is the investigative workspace. It is discarded on close; its conclusions are written to Decision Memory.

Principle 4

Uncertainty Is Explicit

Every Investigation carries an explicit uncertainty state. Known unknowns are listed, confidence floors are enforced at each pipeline gate, and compound uncertainty is tracked and surfaced in governance documentation.

Principle 5

Evidence Decays

Evidence has freshness requirements derived from the rate of change of its subject domain. Evidence that has passed its freshness threshold must be re-acquired before being used to support a Change Proposal.

Principle 6

Everything Is Versioned

Every core object is versioned. The GEAR graph is append-only. Governance decisions are immutable. Evidence is append-only. No platform action permanently destroys or overwrites historical data.

Principle 7

Agents propose; humans decide. No agent has write access to the GEARS Blueprint. Only a Governance Decision, issued by an authorised human, can cause a blueprint update. This constraint is architectural, not procedural.

Section 10 · Agent Taxonomy

Ten Agent Capabilities

The Business Evolution Platform defines ten investigative agent capabilities. Each capability is a bounded role with defined tool access, evidence write permissions, and contribution types. Capabilities can be composed — an Investigation may engage multiple agents simultaneously, each working on a different Architectural Question within the same investigation.

Agents are not named individuals with persistent identity across investigations. They are capability instances — the platform instantiates the appropriate capability for each investigative task and retires it when the task is complete.

Section 11 · Knowledge Integration

Knowledge Providers

Knowledge Providers are the sources through which the Business Evolution Platform populates the Business Knowledge Stack with live, structured evidence. Every provider is accessed through a common evidence interface — ensuring that evidence from different sources carries consistent metadata regardless of origin.

The evidence interface enforces: source identity and authority weight, collection timestamp and freshness decay function, content structure and BKS layer assignment, and conflict registration with other evidence items on the same subject.

Section 12 · Manager UX · Governance

Governance as Decision Architecture

The governance layer of the Business Evolution Platform is not a workflow approval system. It is a decision architecture — the structured environment in which authorised humans review fully evidenced proposals, understand what the investigation found, what it couldn't find, what the simulation stress-tested, and what the agents challenged.

Governance Gate · Four-Stage Review Process · Authority levels by reversibility class

Reversibility Classification

Every Change Proposal is classified by reversibility before governance review. Reversible changes may proceed as Experiments with manager-level authority. Partially reversible changes require senior authority and a defined rollback cost assessment. Irreversible changes require full governance board review with complete simulation documentation.

Dissent as Data

The governance record captures not just the decision but dissenting views — from human participants, from agents whose challenge positions were not addressed, and from simulation runs that flagged conditional risks. Dissent is not overridden; it is recorded alongside the decision and surfaces in the Deployment monitoring plan as risk indicators to watch.

Section 13 · Platform Configuration

Configuring the Evolution Engine

The Business Evolution Platform is configured at eight dimensions. Configuration is versioned, audited, and GEAR-scoped where applicable — configurations that affect specific GEARs are recorded in that GEAR's history.

Configuration Dimensions · Eight axes of platform behaviour · All versioned and audited

GEARSet Configuration

The canonical definition of the GEAR topology — which GEARs exist, how they relate, what their ownership structure is, and what their monitoring thresholds are. This is the primary business configuration and the foundation against which all investigations are scoped.

Trigger Rules

The conditions under which the platform automatically opens an investigation. Trigger rules are configured per GEAR, per metric, and per signal type — with sensitivity thresholds, noise filters, and deduplication windows. Rules are versioned; changes to trigger rules are investigations in themselves.

Governance Policies

The authority levels required for each type of governance decision, mapped to reversibility class. Policies define who can approve what, under what evidence standards, and with what mandatory review steps. Policy changes require senior authority and full audit trail.

Risk Profiles

GEAR-level risk configurations that set confidence thresholds, mandatory simulation requirements, and monitoring sensitivity for specific parts of the GEAR topology. High-criticality GEARs carry more conservative risk profiles than experimental or low-impact ones.

Section 14 · The Manager Experience

A Manager's Morning

The design mandate of the Business Evolution Platform is most clearly expressed by describing what a manager's experience actually looks like — not as a process walkthrough, but as the felt experience of governing an enterprise that is continuously improving itself.

Manager Morning Flow · Attention-ordered governance activity · Not agent management

Sarah opens the platform at 08:30. She does not see a list of agent tasks completed overnight. She sees three things that need her attention: a Change Proposal ready for her review, a Hypothesis that has reached sufficient confidence to draft a proposal but is waiting for her to confirm the blast radius scope, and a Governance Decision she issued two weeks ago whose monitoring observations have now diverged from prediction — triggering a recommendation to open a new investigation.

She spends forty minutes on these three items. The revenue recognition proposal is compelling — the evidence is strong, the blast radius is bounded, the simulation found no failure modes under normal load. She approves it as an Experiment: reversible, time-bounded to 30 days, with an automatic rollback threshold set.

She has not managed an AI agent this morning. She has made three architectural decisions about her enterprise, backed by fourteen days of structured investigation, scored evidence, and simulation stress-testing. That is the purpose of the platform.

Conclusion

The Platform Is the Governance Layer

The Business Evolution Platform described in this paper is the operational realisation of the theory established in White Papers 1 and 2. WP1 defined what an AI-native enterprise knows. WP2 defined how it changes. WP3 defines what it looks like to work inside one — the screens a manager sees, the objects she interacts with, the agents she never has to manage.

The reference architecture is intentionally execution-agnostic. The choice of graph database, agent orchestration framework, knowledge graph technology, or UI stack does not determine whether a platform implements this architecture. What determines that is whether it satisfies the seven principles — particularly the last and most important one: agents propose; humans decide.

The Business Evolution Platform does not make the enterprise autonomous. It makes the enterprise governable — at the speed and scale that AI-native operation demands.

An enterprise that deploys this platform does not hand its architectural evolution to AI. It gains a system that continuously surfaces what it does not yet understand about itself, gathers the evidence to fill those gaps, and presents the results — structured, evidenced, and challenged — to the humans who have the authority and the judgment to act on them.

The best AI-native enterprises will not be the ones where AI makes the most decisions. They will be the ones where humans make better decisions, faster, because the evidence behind every decision has already been rigorously assembled.
