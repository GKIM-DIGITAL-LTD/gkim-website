GEARS White Paper No. 2

The Self-Evolving Enterprise

Designing the Agentic Change Management System

Every improvement begins with an Architectural Inquiry — not a task. The business continuously redesigns itself through evidence-based, negotiated evolution.

Section 1 · Context

From Knowledge Operating System to Change Engine

White Paper No. 1 established that an AI-native enterprise is one that continuously transforms knowledge into improved business design. It introduced the Business Knowledge Stack (BKS) as the layered architecture from which a Living Digital Twin emerges, and described the Observe → Understand → Negotiate → Redesign → Deploy → Learn cycle as the operational rhythm of the self-evolving enterprise.

This paper addresses the question that WP1 left open: what, precisely, does the machinery look like that runs that cycle? How does an observation become a hypothesis? How does a hypothesis become a change proposal? How does a change proposal navigate approval, simulation, and deployment without becoming either a bureaucratic bottleneck or a dangerous source of uncontrolled mutation?

This Paper Introduces

The Agentic Change Management System (ACMS) — the control plane that continuously detects improvement opportunities, conducts architectural inquiry, gathers evidence, negotiates change, and evolves the GEARS Blueprint.

The ACMS is not a workflow engine wrapped around a change management process. It is an architectural inquiry engine: a system whose primary outputs are structured investigations, evidence profiles, and governed architectural decisions — not completed tasks.

Section 2 · Core Thesis

Why Inquiry, Not Tasks

Conventional change management systems are task-oriented. A problem is identified, a solution is proposed, a ticket is opened, a team is assigned, work is done, and the ticket is closed. This model has a structural flaw: it assumes the problem is understood before the work begins.

In a complex adaptive system — which is what any sufficiently large business is — the problem is rarely understood at the moment it is identified. The signal that something is wrong is observed long before its cause is known, its scope is mapped, or its optimal remedy is determined. Acting on early signals without investigation produces expensive, misaimed change.

The ACMS treats every improvement signal as the opening of an investigation, not the assignment of a task. The investigation is the primary object. The task — if any — is its eventual conclusion.

This distinction has architectural consequences that propagate through the entire system. It means the ACMS maintains a structured Evolution Backlog of open investigations at different stages of maturity, not a task queue. It means agents are investigative entities — gathering evidence, forming hypotheses, challenging assumptions — not execution workers. And it means governance acts on evidence-backed proposals, not on gut-feel requests.

Task-Oriented Change

Signal → Problem Statement → Solution → Task → Execution → Done. Fast but shallow. Works for well-understood problems. Systematically fails when the signal is ambiguous, the cause is disputed, or the solution space is large.

Inquiry-Oriented Change

Signal → Investigation → Evidence → Hypotheses → Proposal → Negotiation → Simulation → Governed Decision → Deployment → Learning. Slower but deep. Appropriate for architectural decisions whose cost of reversal is high.

Section 3 · System Architecture

The ACMS Pipeline

The ACMS operates as a fourteen-stage pipeline from trigger to learning. This is not a linear sequence that each change traverses lock-step — it is a state machine. Investigations move forward when evidence justifies it, pause when uncertainty is high, branch when multiple hypotheses require parallel exploration, and are abandoned when the evidence fails to support the original premise.

01
Trigger
A signal enters the system and is assessed for investigation worthiness

Trigger Sources

  • Operational metric anomaly
  • BKS knowledge update (new external intelligence)
  • Scheduled architectural review
  • Human observation or escalation
  • Simulation result divergence

Assessment Criteria

  • Signal strength vs. noise floor
  • Novelty relative to open investigations
  • Strategic relevance weighting
  • Cost of ignoring vs. cost of investigating

Investigation Opened
A structured investigation object is instantiated in the Evolution Backlog

Investigation Object Contains

  • Trigger signal with provenance
  • Initial scope hypothesis
  • Assigned investigative agents
  • Open questions register

State at Opening

  • Status: Active Investigation
  • Evidence: pending
  • Hypotheses: none formed
  • Priority: assessed from trigger
03
Issue Framing
The investigation defines its Architectural Questions — the precise questions that evidence must answer

Architectural Questions

  • Scoped to specific GEAR(s) or cross-cutting concern
  • Expressed as falsifiable propositions
  • Ordered by epistemic dependency

Framing Quality Checks

  • Is this answerable with available evidence types?
  • Is scope correctly bounded?
  • Does this duplicate an open investigation?
04
Target GEAR Identification
The GEAR or GEARs implicated by the investigation are identified and tagged

GEAR Targeting

  • Primary GEAR(s) directly implicated
  • Upstream dependency GEARs
  • Downstream affected GEARs
  • Cross-cutting concern scope if applicable

Why This Matters

  • GEAR ownership assigns investigative authority
  • Determines whose evidence is authoritative
  • Shapes the recursive diagnosis path
05
Recursive GEAR Diagnosis
The investigation descends bidirectionally through the GEAR graph to map causal and consequential scope

Upward Traversal

  • What upstream GEARs are causing this?
  • Where does the causal chain originate?
  • Are root causes architectural or operational?

Downward Traversal

  • What downstream GEARs are affected?
  • What dependent outputs are at risk?
  • What is the blast radius of proposed change?
06
Knowledge Acquisition Strategy
A plan is formed for what evidence is needed, from which BKS layers, in what sequence, and with what freshness requirements

Strategy Dimensions

  • Evidence types required per Architectural Question
  • BKS layer sources to query
  • Evidence freshness thresholds
  • Parallel vs. sequential acquisition

Adaptive Properties

  • Strategy updates as evidence changes the question
  • New questions trigger KAS revision
  • Cost/confidence trade-offs tracked explicitly
07
Evidence Gathering
Investigative agents execute the KAS, pulling from the BKS, external sources, and operational telemetry

Evidence Sources

  • BKS Layers 1–6 per relevance
  • Operational telemetry (L4 evidence)
  • External real-time data
  • Domain expert elicitation

Evidence Metadata

  • Provenance and collection timestamp
  • Freshness score vs. threshold
  • Weighting per Knowledge Weighting model
  • Conflicts flagged for resolution
08
Hypothesis Formation
Evidence is synthesised into one or more competing hypotheses about cause, scope, and remedy

Hypothesis Structure

  • Causal claim with evidence references
  • Scope boundary assertion
  • Proposed remedy class
  • Confidence level (calibrated)

Multiple Hypotheses

  • Competing hypotheses maintained in parallel
  • Evidence discrimination tracked per hypothesis
  • Eliminated hypotheses recorded with reasoning
09
Change Proposal Drafted
The leading hypothesis is formalised into a structured Change Proposal for the GEARS Blueprint

Proposal Contents

  • Architectural change specification
  • Evidence profile (Knowledge Weighting model)
  • Grill-Me framework responses
  • Blast radius assessment

Proposal Metadata

  • Originating investigation ID
  • Rejected alternatives with reasons
  • Reversibility classification
  • Simulation requirements
10
Agentic Negotiation
The Change Proposal enters the multi-participant negotiation surface — humans, AI agents, simulation, domain experts

Negotiation Participants

  • Human stakeholders with governance authority
  • AI agents with domain and evidence access
  • Simulation engines for quantitative challenge
  • Affected GEAR owners with operational insight

Negotiation Outputs

  • Approved as proposed
  • Approved with modifications
  • Returned to investigation with new questions
  • Rejected with documented reasoning
11
Simulation
Approved proposals are stress-tested against simulation models before blueprint update

Simulation Types

  • Financial impact modelling
  • Operational load and capacity simulation
  • Monte Carlo uncertainty analysis
  • Dependency cascade stress testing

Simulation Gate

  • Pass: proceed to governance
  • Conditional: proceed with monitoring plan
  • Fail: return to negotiation with findings
12
Governance Decision
Final authority reviews simulation results and issues a deployment decision with conditions

Decision Inputs

  • Full investigation record
  • Evidence profile and Grill-Me responses
  • Simulation results and conditional flags
  • Negotiation history and dissenting views

Decision Types

  • Full deployment
  • Staged rollout with monitoring gates
  • Experiment (reversible, time-bounded)
  • Deferred (backlogged with conditions)
13
Blueprint Deployment
The GEARS Blueprint is updated and changes propagate through the operational architecture

Deployment Steps

  • Blueprint version incremented
  • Affected GEAR configurations updated
  • Monitoring and alerting thresholds set
  • Rollback plan activated

Propagation Scope

  • Primary GEAR update
  • Upstream/downstream GEAR notifications
  • Documentation and BKS update (L5 Decision Memory)
14
Learning & Memory
The investigation is closed, its full record written to Decision Memory, and new triggers may be spawned

What Is Recorded

  • Complete investigation record
  • All hypotheses with their fates
  • Evidence quality retrospective
  • Negotiation and dissent record

Feedback Loops

  • Deployment outcome triggers monitoring
  • Monitoring anomalies may open new investigations
  • Decision Memory informs future KAS planning
Section 4 · Core Data Structure

The Investigation as Primary Object

The architectural decision to treat the investigation — not the task, not the change request, not the ticket — as the primary object of the system has consequences that ripple through the entire ACMS design. Understanding why the investigation is primary, and what it contains, is essential to understanding how the system behaves.

Investigation Object · Anatomy of a Structured Architectural Inquiry
Trigger Signal
The originating observation — with full provenance metadata: source, timestamp, signal type, confidence, and the BKS layer from which it emerged. Preserved permanently; never overwritten.
Architectural Questions
The precise, falsifiable questions the investigation must answer before it can conclude. Ordered by epistemic dependency — some questions cannot be asked until others are answered. The investigation cannot advance until its questions are defined.
Temporary Working Graph
Each investigation builds its own temporary knowledge sub-graph from the relevant fragments of the BKS, populated as evidence is gathered. This graph is the investigative workspace — it is discarded when the investigation closes, but its conclusions are written to Decision Memory.
Hypothesis Register
All hypotheses formed during investigation — both surviving and eliminated — with their supporting evidence, confidence scores, and (for eliminated hypotheses) the evidence that ruled them out. Competing hypotheses are maintained simultaneously, not sequentially.
Evidence Ledger
Every piece of evidence gathered, with provenance, freshness score, weighting applied, and the Architectural Question it was gathered to answer. Immutable append-only log; contradicting evidence is added, not used to overwrite prior entries.
Uncertainty State
A first-class field, not a footnote. The investigation tracks its current uncertainty level as a structured object: known unknowns listed, confidence levels per hypothesis, evidence gaps identified, and the minimum evidence threshold required to proceed.

The investigation object is designed to be self-planning. As evidence changes what is known, the Architectural Questions are revised, the Knowledge Acquisition Strategy is updated, and the investigation routes itself to the next appropriate stage without requiring external instruction. Agents assigned to an investigation are given authority to revise the plan as well as execute it.

Section 5 · Diagnostic Architecture

Recursive GEAR Diagnosis

One of the structural innovations of the ACMS is its treatment of GEAR diagnosis as a recursive, bidirectional graph traversal — not a flat root-cause analysis performed on a single process.

When an investigation identifies a target GEAR, it does not simply analyse that GEAR in isolation. It traverses the GEAR graph in two directions simultaneously: upstream to find causal predecessors, and downstream to map consequential impact. This bidirectional traversal continues recursively until it either reaches the boundary of the GEAR graph, or encounters a GEAR that does not meet the relevance threshold for inclusion in the investigation scope.

Recursive GEAR Diagnosis · Bidirectional Graph Traversal from Target GEAR

Upward Traversal (Causal)

Moving toward the inputs of the target GEAR — asking: what feeds this GEAR? What earlier design decisions produce the conditions that make this problem possible? Where is the true architectural root?

Traversal stops when: the causal chain reaches a GEAR with no further upstream dependency, or when causal evidence strength drops below threshold.

Downward Traversal (Consequential)

Moving toward the outputs of the target GEAR — asking: what does this GEAR produce that other GEARs consume? What downstream processes, decisions, and customer experiences are at risk if this GEAR is changed?

Traversal stops when: impact probability drops below threshold, or when a downstream GEAR acts as a buffer that absorbs upstream variation.

Key Property

The diagnosis never assumes the problem is where the symptom appears. The target GEAR is the starting point of an investigation, not the conclusion of one.

The recursive diagnosis produces a Blast Radius Map — a subgraph of the GEAR topology that defines the minimum scope of architectural consideration. Any change proposal that does not account for every GEAR in the blast radius map is considered architecturally incomplete and is returned to investigation.

Section 6 · Evidence Architecture

The Knowledge Acquisition Strategy

Every investigation generates a Knowledge Acquisition Strategy — a structured plan that specifies what evidence is needed, from which BKS layers it should be drawn, in what order acquisition should proceed, and what freshness standards each piece of evidence must meet.

The KAS is not a static document. It is an adaptive plan that updates as evidence changes the shape of the investigation. Discoveries made early in evidence gathering may eliminate some Architectural Questions entirely, generate new ones, or shift the relative priority of evidence acquisition tracks.

Knowledge Acquisition Strategy · Evidence Planning Across BKS Layers
Evidence Freshness
Evidence has a freshness requirement derived from the rate of change of its subject domain. Regulatory knowledge may be valid for months; operational telemetry may be stale within hours. The KAS specifies freshness thresholds per evidence type and will re-acquire stale evidence before closing a hypothesis.
Parallel Acquisition Tracks
Where Architectural Questions are epistemically independent — where the answer to one does not change what should be asked of another — the KAS authorises parallel acquisition tracks. This prevents the investigation from becoming a serial bottleneck when multiple independent evidence streams are needed.
Cost-Confidence Trade-off
Not all evidence is equally expensive to acquire. Primary research, domain expert elicitation, and real-time data collection all carry cost. The KAS explicitly models the marginal confidence gain per unit of acquisition cost, and can recommend stopping evidence gathering when the curve flattens.
Evidence Conflict Resolution
When evidence from different sources contradicts, the KAS does not pick a winner by default. It escalates the conflict to the investigation record as a flagged uncertainty, initiates resolution acquisition (seeking a tie-breaking source), and propagates the conflict to the relevant hypothesis confidence scores.
Section 7 · Epistemic Architecture

Uncertainty as First-Class

Most change management systems treat uncertainty as a risk to be managed — something to be reduced before a decision can be made. The ACMS takes a different position: uncertainty is a first-class data type that must be explicitly represented, tracked, and communicated at every stage of an investigation.

This matters because in complex systems, the pretence of certainty is more dangerous than acknowledged uncertainty. A change proposal that claims 85% confidence when it should claim 40% — because the analyst rounded up, because the evidence was stale, or because the scope was underestimated — produces false confidence in the governance layer that approves it. The ACMS is designed to make this pretence structurally difficult.

Known Unknowns

Questions the investigation knows it has not yet answered. Explicitly listed in the investigation object, each with a note on whether a KAS track is open to address it, and what the estimated cost of resolution is.

Unknown Unknowns

Gaps in the GEAR diagnosis scope that the investigation has not yet explored. Flagged by traversal bounds — every time the recursive diagnosis stops short of a complete traversal, the stopping point is logged as a known scope limitation.

Confidence Floors

Each stage of the pipeline has a minimum confidence threshold that must be met before the investigation can advance. A Change Proposal cannot be drafted while competing hypotheses remain within a defined confidence margin of each other — the investigation must first discriminate.

Uncertainty Propagation

Uncertainty compounds through the pipeline. A proposal built on 70% confident evidence that was itself built on 80% confident diagnosis carries a compound confidence lower than either. The ACMS tracks this compounding explicitly and surfaces it in governance documentation.

Design Principle

An investigation that cannot characterise its own uncertainty is not ready to produce a Change Proposal. Uncertainty is not a sign of weakness in an investigation — it is a sign of honesty. The governance layer should distrust proposals that claim no uncertainty more than proposals that quantify it clearly.

Section 8 · Governance Dynamics

Agentic Negotiation

When a Change Proposal exits investigation and enters negotiation, it crosses from the domain of inquiry into the domain of decision. The negotiation surface is where competing interests, interpretations, and authority levels meet — and where the ACMS must prevent the degeneration of structured evidence-based discussion into either rubber-stamping or impasse.

Agentic Negotiation Surface · Multi-participant governed decision process

Each participant in negotiation has a defined role, a defined scope of authority, and a defined contribution type. Participants cannot veto based on preference alone — every objection must be grounded in evidence, scope, or authority, and must be accompanied by a specific, answerable counter-question or a concrete alternative proposal.

Human Stakeholders

Authority & Context

Provide strategic context, governance authority, and organisational memory that may not be captured in the formal evidence record. Final approval authority rests here.

AI Investigative Agents

Evidence & Challenge

Present the investigation record, surface evidence conflicts, challenge weak causal claims, and propose alternative hypotheses that were considered and rejected.

Simulation Engines

Quantitative Stress

Run real-time scenario challenges against the proposal during negotiation, surfacing quantitative risks that the qualitative discussion may have underweighted.

Section 9 · Decision Architecture

Governance & Simulation

Governance in the ACMS is not a final approval step bolted onto the end of a change process. It is an architectural function that runs throughout the pipeline — setting confidence thresholds, defining traversal bounds, approving investigation scope extensions, and ultimately ratifying or rejecting Change Proposals with full accountability.

Staged Governance

Governance touches the pipeline at four points: investigation scope approval (before significant evidence acquisition begins), hypothesis approval (before a Change Proposal is drafted), simulation gate (after simulation results are available), and final deployment decision. Each gate has defined authority levels and documented criteria.

Reversibility as a Governance Variable

The ACMS classifies every Change Proposal by its reversibility: fully reversible, partially reversible (with defined rollback cost), or effectively irreversible. Reversibility classification directly determines the governance authority level required — irreversible changes require higher authority and more complete evidence profiles than reversible experiments.

Governance Principle

The governance layer does not make the investigation's decision for it. It sets the standards by which the investigation's conclusion will be evaluated — and it is accountable for those standards, not just the decisions made against them.

After governance approval, every non-trivial change passes through a simulation gate before blueprint deployment. The simulation gate is not a validation of the change against the current model — it is a stress test. Simulation engines are instructed to find the conditions under which the proposed change fails, not the conditions under which it succeeds. A simulation that only confirms the expected outcome adds little value; one that identifies a failure mode under a specific load pattern or dependency configuration adds substantial value to the governance decision record.

Section 10 · System Dynamics

The Evolution Backlog

The Evolution Backlog is the ACMS's view of all investigations currently in flight — across all fourteen pipeline stages, at all levels of maturity, covering all parts of the GEAR graph. It is the primary interface between the ACMS and the humans who govern it.

Unlike a conventional task backlog, the Evolution Backlog is not simply a priority-ordered list of work to be done. It is a portfolio of open hypotheses — a live map of every question the organisation is currently investigating about its own architecture, together with each question's evidence state, uncertainty level, and distance from a governance decision.

Evolution Backlog · Live Portfolio of Open Investigations by Stage and Priority
Investigation Priority
Priority is not simply urgency. It is a composite of strategic relevance, evidence maturity (how close is the investigation to a conclusion?), cost of delay, and blast radius of the underlying issue. The backlog re-prioritises continuously as these factors change.
Investigation Merging
When two open investigations converge on the same GEAR, the same causal hypothesis, or a shared evidence requirement, the backlog management function detects the overlap and proposes an investigation merge. This prevents the system from running parallel investigations into the same architectural question at different evidence maturity levels.
Investigation Spawning
An investigation can spawn child investigations when its recursive diagnosis identifies a GEAR that merits separate investigation at greater depth, or when a hypothesis generates a secondary question that cannot be resolved within the parent investigation's scope. Child investigations are linked in the record.
Stale Investigation Management
An investigation that has not progressed in a defined window is flagged as stale. Stale investigations either receive a scheduled reactivation (when their triggering signal is expected to recur), are merged into a related active investigation, or are formally closed as inconclusive — with reasons documented in Decision Memory.
Section 11 · Synthesis

Emergent Properties

When all fourteen pipeline stages, the investigation object, the recursive diagnosis, the adaptive KAS, the first-class uncertainty model, and the multi-participant negotiation surface operate together as a coherent system, several important properties emerge at the system level — properties that none of the individual components possesses alone.

Property 1

Architectural Self-Awareness

The ACMS builds and continuously updates a structured understanding of the GEAR graph's own weaknesses, its evidence gaps, and the questions its investigations have not yet been able to answer. The organisation learns where it doesn't know what it doesn't know.

Property 2

Governed Autonomy

Investigative agents operate autonomously within the bounds of their KAS — gathering evidence, forming hypotheses, updating plans — without requiring human instruction at each step. But every consequential decision is governed. Autonomy is scoped to investigation; authority over deployment remains human.

Property 3

Institutional Memory Compounding

Every investigation that closes writes to Decision Memory — not just the outcome, but the full record of what was asked, what was found, what was rejected, and why. Over time, this record becomes a compounding asset: future investigations run faster, with fewer blind alleys, because the organisation remembers having been there before.

Property 4

Evidence-Based Culture

The structural requirement that every Change Proposal arrive with a full evidence profile, Grill-Me responses, and a blast radius assessment creates cultural pressure toward evidence-based reasoning. Proposals without evidence are not refused — they are returned to investigation until they have it.

Property 5

Continuous Improvement Velocity

As the Decision Memory fills and the investigation process matures, the ACMS becomes more efficient. Hypotheses that match prior investigations are identified earlier. Evidence that was expensive to acquire once is available immediately for future investigations. The cost per improvement declines as the system matures.

Property 6

Blast Radius Discipline

The mandatory recursive diagnosis and blast radius assessment mean that narrow proposals — those that fix a symptom without understanding its cause or its downstream effects — cannot survive the investigation process. The system structurally prevents tunnel-vision change.

Conclusion

An Inquiry Engine, Not a Workflow Engine

The Agentic Change Management System described in this paper is a fundamentally different kind of enterprise software from the change management, ITSM, and workflow platforms it superficially resembles. Those systems ask: what work needs to be done, and by whom? The ACMS asks: what do we not yet understand about our own architecture, and what would it take to know?

This shift in the primary question — from task completion to architectural inquiry — is not cosmetic. It produces a system with different failure modes, different incentives, and different long-term properties. A task management system fails by losing track of work. An inquiry engine fails by losing track of questions.

The ACMS is not merely a workflow engine that automates change management. It is an architectural inquiry engine that serves the GEARS Blueprint — one whose primary output is not completed tasks, but structured understanding.

The organisation that deploys the ACMS is not simply automating its change process. It is building institutional capacity to continuously ask better questions about its own architecture — and to govern the answers rigorously enough that the improvements they produce are trusted, durable, and compounding.

The business that never stops questioning its own design never becomes obsolete. The ACMS is the mechanism through which that questioning is made rigorous, governed, and continuous.
