How we think about fees

Capacity-based, not activity-based

Our fees reflect the operational infrastructure we reserve for you — staffing, platform capacity, workflow configuration, and account management. They are never calculated by reference to test volumes, referrals, claims, or clinical outcomes.

⚙

Reserved capacity

Your monthly fee secures dedicated platform resources, operational staffing, and workflow readiness — whether you are at peak volume or ramping up.

⬡

Workflow complexity

Fees scale with operational intensity — the number of workflow steps, patient touchpoints, data routing requirements, and coordination burden — not with downstream clinical activity.

↺

Prospective adjustments only

Fee changes follow a structured review cycle and apply going forward only. There are no retrospective true-ups to clinical events, tests ordered, or reimbursement received.

✕

No referral economics

Compensation is commercially reasonable in the absence of any referral or clinical activity. No element of our fee is tied to the value or volume of healthcare programme business.

Appendix B — Service Classification Framework

Four operational service classes

Workflows are classified by operational complexity — support intensity, coordination burden, exception rate, and routing requirements. Classification is an operational assessment, not a clinical one.

Class Profile Typical operational characteristics
Class A Standard workflow Low-touch, primarily automated. Simple intake processing, basic status routing, low exception rate, minimal provider coordination. Platform automation carries most of the operational burden.
Class B Moderate workflow Multi-step workflows requiring recurring coordination and moderate patient engagement. Includes scheduling support, reminders, moderate document handling, operational follow-up, and routine exceptions.
Class C Complex workflow High-touch with multiple patient interactions, kit logistics coordination, provider front desk support, result routing, rescheduling, exception handling, and escalations.
Class D Intensive workflow Longitudinal, multi-provider, or high-escalation workflows requiring sustained coordination over time. Repeated interactions, multi-stage follow-up, ongoing monitoring, complex data routing, and periodic status management.

Review and adjustment mechanism

A structured path from pilot to partnership

Every engagement begins with a two-week pilot, followed by a structured review cadence. Each review is an opportunity to align capacity, class mix, and fees to actual operational demand — always prospectively.

Weeks 1–2
Pilot phase
Live workflow testing at agreed capacity. We validate intake routing, consent verification, platform configuration, and operational throughput before locking in the full commercial structure. Either party may raise concerns before the pilot concludes.
Month 1
Initial review
First operational review following go-live. We assess actual workflow volume, support demand, exception rates, and service class fit. Adjustments to capacity tier or service class mix can be agreed and applied from the following month.
Month 3
Stability review
Broader review of operational performance, staffing allocation, and infrastructure utilisation. A good point to confirm or adjust the service class distribution and fee structure as volumes stabilise.
Month 6
Mid-term review
Comprehensive review of the full engagement. Scope, capacity, service class assumptions, and commercial terms are reassessed in light of six months of operational data. Changes are documented in a written amendment.
Ongoing
Periodic reviews
Reviews continue at agreed intervals beyond Month 6. All adjustments are prospective — no retrospective true-ups, no adjustments tied to clinical events, reimbursement activity, or test volumes.
Compliance foundation

Built around clinical independence

GKIM Digital provides technology and operational services only. The boundaries below are structural, not optional, and are embedded in every Order Form.

⊘

No clinical decisions

We do not determine medical necessity, select tests, direct referrals, or influence provider judgment. All clinical decisions rest solely with the responsible Clinical Provider.

⊘

No volume incentives

No quota, target, or referral obligation applies. Minimum commitments represent reserved operational capacity, not a requirement to generate clinical activity.

✓

Consent integrity

Client is responsible for lawful data collection and all required consents. We may require TrustedForm or equivalent provenance records before processing any patient record.

✓

FMV documentation

We maintain internal fair market value documentation — cost models, staffing assumptions, infrastructure costs, and market comparables — to support the commercial reasonableness of our fees.

The plain version

We are paid for the operational work we do, not for what happens clinically downstream. Our fees are set before any clinical activity takes place and would be the same whether no tests were ever ordered or all of them were. That is the model, and it is the same for every partner.
